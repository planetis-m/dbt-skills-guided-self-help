# Clinical Basis

Use this reference to understand which parts of DBT can be adapted as bounded
guided self-help. It summarizes sources; it is not a DBT treatment manual.

## Design Conclusions

- Comprehensive DBT is an intensive, structured psychological treatment, not a
  brief self-help conversation.
- Full DBT commonly combines individual therapy, skills training, between-
  session coaching, and a clinician consultation team.
- DBT balances acceptance and change. Its four main skills modules are
  mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, and interpersonal
  effectiveness.
- Mindfulness supports present-moment awareness and observing or describing
  experience without immediately reacting.
- Emotion regulation includes noticing and naming emotions, checking facts,
  noticing action urges, and selecting a response that fits the situation and
  the person's goals.
- Distress tolerance helps a person get through painful moments without making
  them worse; it does not solve the underlying problem.
- Interpersonal effectiveness concerns asking for what one wants, saying no,
  and balancing objectives, relationships, and self-respect.
- DBT skills training alone is promising across clinical and nonclinical
  settings, but evidence does not make an AI interaction equivalent to DBT
  treatment or clinician-led skills training.
- Technology-mediated DBT remains an area where more research is needed to
  determine which elements help, for whom, and under what conditions.

## AI Adaptation

The skill adapts low-risk, repeatable elements:

- a dialectical acceptance-and-change stance
- mindful observing and describing
- basic emotion labeling, fact checking, and urge awareness
- ordinary non-crisis distress tolerance
- rehearsal of safe requests, refusals, or boundaries
- one small human-chosen practice plan and a progress review

It does not adapt comprehensive DBT treatment, clinical target hierarchy,
formal chain analysis, crisis coaching, risk management, contingency
management, trauma treatment, or treatment for severe or specialized
conditions.

## Sources

Accessed 2026-06-04.

1. University of Washington Behavioral Research and Therapy Clinics,
   **Dialectical Behavior Therapy**: describes DBT's primary acceptance-and-
   change dialectic, four skills modules, treatment targets, comprehensive
   treatment modes, and promising evidence for skills training alone.
   <https://depts.washington.edu/uwbrtc/about-us/dialectical-behavior-therapy/>

2. Dorset HealthCare University NHS Foundation Trust, **Dialectical behaviour
   therapy**: describes comprehensive DBT's components, four skills modules,
   acceptance-and-change balance, and promising evidence for skills training
   alone.
   <https://www.dorsethealthcare.nhs.uk/our-services-and-sites/mental-health-and-learning-disabilities/intensive-psychological-therapies/dialectical-behaviour-therapy-dbt>

3. NICE, **Borderline personality disorder: recognition and management
   (CG78)**: describes DBT as a comprehensive programme and advises against
   brief psychological interventions specifically for borderline personality
   disorder outside an appropriately structured service.
   <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg78/chapter/1-Guidance>

4. NICE, **Dialectical behaviour therapy glossary entry**: describes DBT as an
   intensive treatment that usually takes place over one year with weekly
   individual and group meetings.
   <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg78/ifp/chapter/glossary>

5. Manchester University NHS Foundation Trust CAMHS, **Emotional Regulation**:
   describes noticing and naming emotions, checking facts, identifying action
   urges, choosing a strategy, and reflecting afterward.
   <https://mft.nhs.uk/camhs/advice/emotional-regulation>

6. NHS Every Mind Matters, **What is mindfulness?**: describes present-moment
   attention, awareness of thoughts, stepping back from them, and naming them.
   <https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/what-is-mindfulness/>

7. Yale Medicine, **Dialectical Behavior Therapy**: summarizes mindfulness,
   distress tolerance, emotion regulation, and interpersonal effectiveness,
   including asking, saying no, and maintaining relationships and self-respect.
   <https://www.yalemedicine.org/conditions/dialectical-behavior-therapy-dbt>

8. Rizvi and colleagues, **A systematic review of dialectical behavior therapy
   mobile apps for content and usability**: describes DBT as a complex therapy
   and concludes that more research is needed on when, for whom, and which
   technology-delivered DBT elements are useful.
   <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8639404/>
